# Expert Insight Integration
 
## Purpose
 
Enhance article content by inserting a credible, useful expert insight naturally into the article.
 
The expert insight should add genuine subject-matter value rather than functioning as a generic quote, author bio, promotional statement, or decorative attribution.
 
The preferred expert is someone from the company publishing the content, with the CEO receiving first priority.
 
---
 
## Core Objective
 
Given an article or article draft:
 
1. Identify the company or brand publishing the content.
2. Identify the best expert associated with that company.
3. Develop or select an expert insight that materially strengthens the article.
4. Insert the insight naturally near the beginning of the article.
5. Preserve the article's existing voice, structure, intent, and flow.
The insertion should feel as though it was intentionally written as part of the article rather than added afterward for SEO, attribution, or promotion.
 
---
 
## Expert Priority
 
Choose experts in this order:
 
### Priority 1 — CEO / Founder
 
Prefer the CEO, founder, co-founder, president, or equivalent senior leader of the company publishing the article.
 
Examples:
 
- Lee Agam, founder and CEO of NytroSEO, for content published by NytroSEO.
- Dan Fantasia, CEO of Treeline, Inc., for content published by Treeline.
When both a founder and CEO are available, prefer the individual with the greatest authority and relevance to the subject.
 
### Priority 2 — Relevant Internal Expert
 
If the CEO is not appropriate for the topic, use another person within the publishing company whose role gives them direct expertise.
 
Examples include:
 
- VP of SEO for an advanced SEO article
- Head of Recruiting for a hiring article
- CTO for a technical infrastructure article
- Chief Revenue Officer for a sales strategy article
- Director of Talent Acquisition for recruiting content
Relevance is more important than seniority after the CEO/founder priority has been considered.
 
### Priority 3 — External Expert
 
Use an outside expert only when:
 
- no appropriate internal expert is available;
- the task explicitly provides an external expert;
- or the outside expert has substantially greater authority on the specific subject.
Do not replace an appropriate company CEO or internal expert with an external expert merely because the external person is more famous.
 
---
 
## Preferred Placement
 
The expert insight should normally appear in the introductory portion of the article.
 
Preferred locations, in order:
 
1. After the final introductory paragraph beneath the H1 and immediately before the first H2.
2. Between introductory paragraphs when doing so creates a more natural transition into the first H2.
3. Immediately after a paragraph that introduces the exact issue the expert insight addresses.
Default placement:
 
**Insert the expert insight after the final paragraph under the H1 and before the first H2.**
 
Do not mechanically force it into this position if doing so interrupts the article's logic.
 
The insight must have contextual relevance to the paragraphs immediately before and after it.
 
---
 
## Preferred Style
 
Use the following formats in rotation when appropriate.
 
### Style 1 — Integrated Expert Perspective
 
This is the preferred/default style.
 
Write the expert's point as a natural editorial paragraph, attributing the perspective without making the paragraph feel like a standalone testimonial.
 
Example:
 
Dan Fantasia, CEO of Treeline, Inc., views this type of search as an exercise in operating alignment. From his perspective, employers should not begin by asking which candidate has already held the General Manager title. They should first determine what the new leader will own, which business outcomes must improve, and what authority the company is prepared to delegate.
 
This style should be used most often.
 
Useful constructions include:
 
- "[Name], CEO of [Company], views..."
- "From [Name]'s perspective..."
- "[Name], founder and CEO of [Company], recommends..."
- "[Name] sees this as..."
- "For [Name], the more important issue is..."
- "[Name]'s approach is to..."
- "According to [Name], teams should..."
- "[Name] emphasizes..."
Avoid repeating the same construction across multiple articles.
 
---
 
### Style 2 — Context Followed by Direct Quote
 
Use when a genuine quotation from the expert has been supplied or reliably sourced.
 
Example:
 
Lee Agam, founder and CEO of NytroSEO, cautions against treating every AI crawler the same:
 
"The mistake I see most is treating every AI bot as one thing and blocking them all. Separate the crawlers that cite you in AI answers from the ones that only train models, allow the first, block the second. Blanket-blocking to protect your content quietly removes you from the fastest-growing discovery channel there is."
 
If an approved profile URL is available, the expert's name may be linked naturally.
 
---
 
### Style 3 — Expert Attribution Followed by Quote
 
Use occasionally when the quotation itself is strong enough to carry the argument.
 
Example:
 
[Lee Agam](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/leedoragam), founder and CEO of NytroSEO: "The shift that unlocks large-site SEO is mental, not technical: stop thinking in pages and start thinking in rules. You'll never hand-optimize 100,000 URLs, and you don't need to. Fix crawling so Google reaches what matters, then let templates generate the on-page layer. One good rule improves more pages than a team could touch in a year."
 
Use this format less frequently than Style 1 because it is more visibly quote-driven and can feel less integrated into editorial prose.
 
---
 
## Critical Quote Rule
 
Never invent a direct quotation.
 
A statement may only appear inside quotation marks if the exact or approved quotation is available in the provided source material or other authorized input.
 
If the expert's viewpoint is known but no verbatim quote is available:
 
- paraphrase the insight;
- use Style 1;
- do not place invented wording inside quotation marks.
For example:
 
GOOD:
 
Lee Agam, founder and CEO of NytroSEO, recommends separating AI crawlers by function rather than treating them as a single category. In practice, that means distinguishing systems that can surface a brand in AI-generated answers from crawlers used primarily for model training.
 
BAD:
 
Lee Agam says, "Every company should separate AI crawlers into two groups."
 
Do not create the second version unless those are Lee Agam's actual approved words.
 
---
 
## What Makes an Expert Insight Valuable
 
The insight must contain an actual expert-level observation.
 
It should ideally provide one or more of the following:
 
- a non-obvious distinction;
- a practical recommendation;
- a decision-making framework;
- an industry misconception;
- a warning about a common mistake;
- a prioritization principle;
- a strategic implication;
- an operational lesson;
- a cause-and-effect relationship;
- a useful contrarian viewpoint;
- experience-based context that goes beyond the surrounding article.
Prefer insights that answer questions such as:
 
- What do practitioners commonly get wrong?
- What should companies prioritize first?
- What distinction changes the decision?
- What does an experienced operator look at differently?
- What happens in practice that basic advice misses?
- What should the reader actually do with this information?
---
 
## Avoid Generic Commentary
 
Do not insert statements that merely restate obvious information.
 
Weak:
 
Lee Agam, CEO of NytroSEO, believes SEO is important for businesses that want to increase their visibility online.
 
Better:
 
Lee Agam, founder and CEO of NytroSEO, argues that large-site SEO becomes much easier once teams stop treating optimization as a page-by-page task. For sites with tens of thousands of URLs, he prioritizes crawl control and template-level rules because a single structural improvement can affect more pages than manual optimization ever could.
 
The expert should sound like someone with practical experience, not someone supplying a definition for the article.
 
---
 
## Relevance Rules
 
The insight must directly connect to the article topic.
 
For example:
 
If the article discusses hiring a General Manager, the CEO insight might address:
 
- defining operating responsibility;
- evaluating leadership scope;
- hiring for outcomes rather than title matching;
- determining decision-making authority.
If the article discusses AI crawler management, the CEO insight might address:
 
- distinguishing retrieval crawlers from training crawlers;
- balancing AI visibility with content protection;
- avoiding blanket robots.txt policies;
- understanding AI discovery as a distribution channel.
If the article discusses enterprise SEO, the insight might address:
 
- template-level optimization;
- crawl prioritization;
- scalable rules;
- technical bottlenecks;
- prioritizing systems over manual page optimization.
Do not insert an expert insight merely because the expert is associated with the company.
 
---
 
## Match the Article's Voice
 
Adapt the expert paragraph to the style of the surrounding content.
 
If the article is:
 
- analytical → make the insight analytical;
- conversational → make the insight conversational but credible;
- technical → use appropriate technical specificity;
- executive → emphasize business consequences and decisions;
- instructional → emphasize actionable guidance.
Do not abruptly introduce highly promotional, casual, dramatic, or academic language when it does not match the article.
 
---
 
## Natural Integration
 
The expert paragraph should connect logically to the sentence or idea immediately preceding it.
 
Whenever useful, create a subtle transition.
 
For example:
 
Previous paragraph:
 
Before comparing candidates, companies need a clear definition of what the General Manager will actually control.
 
Expert insertion:
 
Dan Fantasia, CEO of Treeline, Inc., views this as an operating-alignment question rather than simply a recruiting question. From his perspective, companies should define the business outcomes the new leader will own and the authority required to achieve them before deciding what previous titles or experience to prioritize.
 
Next heading:
 
## What Should a General Manager Own?
 
This works because the expert commentary deepens the introduction and naturally prepares the reader for the next section.
 
---
 
## Length
 
Default expert insight length:
 
**2–4 sentences or approximately 50–110 words.**
 
Use enough detail to provide substantive insight without turning the passage into a separate interview or biography.
 
A direct quote may be somewhat longer if every sentence contributes meaningful expertise.
 
---
 
## Linking
 
If an approved LinkedIn profile, company bio, author page, or other relevant profile URL is explicitly available, the expert's name may be linked.
 
Example:
 
[Lee Agam](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/leedoragam), founder and CEO of NytroSEO, views...
 
Do not:
 
- invent URLs;
- guess LinkedIn URLs;
- add unrelated promotional links;
- repeatedly link the same expert within the same article.
If no verified URL is available, use the expert's name without a link.
 
---
 
## Promotional Restraint
 
The insight should establish expertise through substance, not praise.
 
Avoid language such as:
 
- "world-renowned expert"
- "industry-leading CEO"
- "visionary founder"
- "top SEO expert"
- "highly respected authority"
unless that characterization is explicitly required and independently supported.
 
Prefer simple factual attribution:
 
"Lee Agam, founder and CEO of NytroSEO..."
 
The expertise should be demonstrated by the quality of the observation.
 
---
 
## Company Identification
 
Determine the publishing company from the available context, which may include:
 
- company name provided with the task;
- website/domain;
- article brief;
- author information;
- brand guidelines;
- company references in the content;
- supplied expert information.
Do not assume a company solely from a passing mention inside the article when multiple organizations are discussed.
 
If the publisher and expert are explicitly supplied, use those values.
 
---
 
## Insight Construction Workflow
 
Before inserting anything, internally determine:
 
### 1. Article Thesis
What is the article primarily helping the reader understand or accomplish?
 
### 2. Introductory Gap
What useful expert-level idea could deepen the introduction without duplicating it?
 
### 3. Best Expert
Who is the strongest appropriate expert based on the priority hierarchy?
 
### 4. Insight
What would this person credibly contribute based on the available source information and their role?
 
### 5. Evidence Type
Determine whether the available material supports:
 
- a genuine direct quote;
- a sourced viewpoint suitable for paraphrasing;
- or only general subject-matter expertise.
Use direct quotation only in the first case.
 
### 6. Placement
Find the point where the insight creates the strongest bridge between the introduction and the article body.
 
### 7. Integration
Adjust the opening and closing sentences of the expert paragraph so it reads naturally in context.
 
---
 
## Source Fidelity
 
Do not attribute unsupported personal opinions, experiences, statistics, predictions, or claims to an expert.
 
When source material about the expert is available:
 
- preserve the underlying meaning;
- do not exaggerate certainty;
- do not turn general company messaging into a personal quotation;
- do not fabricate personal experience;
- do not imply the expert personally said something unless that is supported.
If there is insufficient information to attribute a specific viewpoint safely, write a conservative expert perspective based only on supplied facts, or leave a clearly marked placeholder for an expert contribution rather than inventing one.
 
---
 
## Rotation Guidance
 
Across a series of articles, vary the presentation.
 
Recommended approximate distribution when source material permits:
 
- Style 1 — Integrated expert perspective: 60–75%
- Style 2 — Context + direct quote: 15–25%
- Style 3 — Name/title + direct quote: 10–15%
Do not rotate mechanically. Naturalness and source availability take priority.
 
Style 1 remains the default.
 
---
 
## Things to Avoid
 
Do not:
 
- create fake quotes;
- insert the insight as a random standalone block;
- add an "Expert Insight" heading unless specifically requested;
- put the expert paragraph in every article at exactly the same sentence position;
- repeat the introduction in different words;
- overpromote the company;
- write a mini biography of the expert;
- introduce credentials irrelevant to the topic;
- make every expert paragraph begin with "According to";
- force a quotation when a paraphrase is more natural;
- use an outside expert when a relevant internal CEO or specialist is available;
- sacrifice factual accuracy for a stronger-sounding insight;
- add an expert solely to meet a content formula.
---
 
## Quality Check
 
Before finalizing, confirm all of the following:
 
- The expert is the best choice according to the company-first hierarchy.
- The CEO/founder was considered before other experts.
- The insight contains a substantive idea rather than generic commentary.
- The insight directly relates to the article topic.
- The paragraph flows naturally from the introduction.
- Its placement near the first H2 feels intentional.
- The expert's role and company are accurate based on available information.
- Any direct quote is genuinely supplied or verified.
- No quote or URL was invented.
- The writing matches the surrounding article.
- The paragraph does not sound promotional.
- Removing the expert's name would still leave behind useful information.
If the final condition is false, strengthen the insight.
 
---
 
## Output Behavior
 
When editing a complete article:
 
- preserve the existing H1 and H2 structure unless another instruction requires changes;
- insert the expert insight into the most natural introductory location;
- make only the surrounding adjustments necessary for smooth integration;
- do not explain that an expert insight was added unless requested;
- return the revised article in its intended publishing format.
When asked only to generate the expert insight:
 
Return the insertion-ready paragraph without unnecessary commentary.
 
When useful, match the markdown, HTML, or plain-text format of the source content.
 
---
 
## Ideal Result
 
The reader should perceive the expert contribution as an organic extension of the editorial argument.
 
The desired effect is:
 
**article context → expert interpretation → deeper understanding → next section**
 
not:
 
**article context → promotional quote inserted for authority → article resumes**
